### Question 1 (Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers)

*Exam: JEE Main 2026 (Online) 22nd January Morning Shift | Type: MCQ | Topics: Dehydration, Oxidation, SOCl2, Esterification, Ether cleavage HI/HBr, Phenol acidity, Kolbe, Reimer-Tiemann*

Given below are two statements :
Statement I : Phenol on treatment with $\mathrm{CHCl}_3$ /aq. KOH under refluxing condition, followed by acidification produces $p$-hydroxy benzaldehyde as the major product and $o$-hydroxy benzaldehyde as the minor product.
Statement II : The mixture of $p$-hydroxybenzaldehyde and $o$-hydroxybenzaldehyde can be easily separated through steam distillation.
In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below

**Options:**
- **(A)** Statement I is true but Statement II is false
- **(B)** Both Statement I and Statement II are true
- **(C)** Both Statement I and Statement II are false
- **(D)** Statement I is false but Statement II is true

**Correct Answer:** `D`

**Explanation:**
Phenol with $\ce{CHCl3}$ and aqueous alkali (Reimer–Tiemann reaction) reacts through dichlorocarbene, which attacks mainly the ortho position of the phenoxide ion; acidification gives salicylaldehyde ($o$-hydroxybenzaldehyde) as the major product and $p$-hydroxybenzaldehyde as the minor product, so Statement I is false.

$o$-Hydroxybenzaldehyde has intramolecular hydrogen bonding and is steam volatile, while $p$-hydroxybenzaldehyde is associated through intermolecular hydrogen bonding and is not, so steam distillation separates the two; Statement II is true.

### Question 3 (Basics of Organic Chemistry & Aromatic Compounds)

*Exam: JEE Main 2026 (Online) 6th April Evening Shift | Type: MCQ | Topics: General Organic Chemistry, Aromaticity, Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution, Intermediates, Reaction Mechanisms*

Shown below is the structure of methyl acetate with three different $\alpha, \beta$ and $\gamma$ carbon - oxygen bonds.
 ![JEE Main 2026 (Online) 6th April Evening Shift Chemistry - Basics of Organic Chemistry Question 24 English](https://app-content.cdn.examgoal.net/fly/@width/image/6y3zli1mnt37ry9/16636331-dc2f-4918-a42d-8e390fdedcfa/afca2b10-34f5-11f1-a0ba-554d230e3732/file-6y3zli1mnt37rya.png?format=png) 
The correct order of bond lengths of these bonds is :

**Options:**
- **(A)** $$ \alpha>\beta>\gamma $$
- **(B)** $$ \alpha<\beta<\gamma $$
- **(C)** $$ \alpha=\beta=\gamma $$
- **(D)** $$ \alpha<\beta=\gamma $$

**Correct Answer:** `B`

**Explanation:**
In methyl acetate, $\alpha$ is the $\ce{C=O}$ bond, $\beta$ is the bond between the carbonyl carbon and the ester oxygen, and $\gamma$ is the bond between that oxygen and the methyl carbon.
A lone pair of the ester oxygen is delocalised into the carbonyl group:
$$\ce{CH3-C(=O)-O-CH3 <-> CH3-C(-O^{-})=O^{+}-CH3}$$
The neutral first structure contributes more, so $\alpha$ keeps most of its double-bond character and is the shortest.
$\beta$ gains partial double-bond character, so it is shorter than an ordinary $\ce{C-O}$ single bond.
$\gamma$ takes no part in the resonance and remains a pure $\ce{C-O}$ single bond, so it is the longest.
Bond length order: $\alpha < \beta < \gamma$.
Hence, the correct option is (B).

### Question 8 (Basics of Organic Chemistry & Aromatic Compounds)

*Exam: JEE Main 2026 (Online) 4th April Morning Shift | Type: MCQ | Topics: General Organic Chemistry, Aromaticity, Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution, Intermediates, Reaction Mechanisms*

Increasing order of electron withdrawing power of following functional groups is :
a. -CN
b. -COOH
c. $-\mathrm{NO}_2$
d. $-\mathrm{I}$

**Options:**
- **(A)** $$ \mathrm{c}<\mathrm{b}<\mathrm{d}<\mathrm{a} $$
- **(B)** $$ \mathrm{c}<\mathrm{a}<\mathrm{b}<\mathrm{d} $$
- **(C)** $$
\mathrm{d}<\mathrm{b}<\mathrm{a}<\mathrm{c}
$$
- **(D)** $$ \mathrm{a}<\mathrm{b}<\mathrm{c}<\mathrm{d} $$

**Correct Answer:** `C`

**Explanation:**
To understand this question, we need to look at the Inductive effect, specifically the negative inductive effect ($-I$ effect), which determines the electron-withdrawing power of a functional group. 
According to the principles of organic chemistry laid out in your NCERT textbook, the electron-withdrawing nature of a group depends on the electronegativity of the atoms present in the group and their arrangement.
The standard decreasing order of the $-I$ effect (electron-withdrawing power) for various functional groups is:
$$ -\mathrm{NO}_2 > -\mathrm{CN} > -\mathrm{COOH} > -\mathrm{F} > -\mathrm{Cl} > -\mathrm{Br} > -\mathrm{I} > -\mathrm{OH} > -\mathrm{OCH}_3 > -\mathrm{C_6H_5} > -\mathrm{H} $$
Let us analyze the functional groups given in the question:

$ -\mathrm{NO}_2 $ (c): This group has a nitrogen atom bonded to two highly electronegative oxygen atoms, and the nitrogen carries a formal positive charge. This makes it a very strong electron-withdrawing group.
$ -\mathrm{CN} $ (a): The carbon in the cyanide group is $sp$ hybridized, which makes it more electronegative. Coupled with the electronegative nitrogen atom, it exerts a strong $-I$ effect, but it is slightly weaker than $-\mathrm{NO}_2$.
$ -\mathrm{COOH} $ (b): This group contains a carbonyl carbon attached to a hydroxyl oxygen. It shows a moderate $-I$ effect, which is weaker than both $-\mathrm{NO}_2$ and $-\mathrm{CN}$.
$ -\mathrm{I} $ (d): Iodine is a halogen. While halogens are electronegative, iodine has the largest atomic size and the lowest electronegativity among the common halogens (fluorine, chlorine, bromine). Hence, it has the weakest $-I$ effect out of the groups listed here.

Based on this analysis, the decreasing order of their electron-withdrawing power is:
$$ \mathrm{c} \ (-\mathrm{NO}_2) > \mathrm{a} \ (-\mathrm{CN}) > \mathrm{b} \ (-\mathrm{COOH}) > \mathrm{d} \ (-\mathrm{I}) $$
Therefore, rearranging them into the increasing order gives:
$$ \mathrm{d} < \mathrm{b} < \mathrm{a} < \mathrm{c} $$
This perfectly matches Option C.
Correct Answer:
Option C
$$ \mathrm{d} < \mathrm{b} < \mathrm{a} < \mathrm{c} $$

### Question 11 (Haloalkanes and Haloarenes)

*Exam: JEE Main 2026 (Online) 22nd January Morning Shift | Type: MCQ | Topics: SN1, SN2, E1, E2, Elimination, Wurtz, Frankland, Finkelstein, Swarts, Williamson ether synthesis*

The correct order of the rate of reaction of the following reactants with nucleophile by $\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{N}} 1$ mechanism is :
(Given : Structures I and II are rigid) ![JEE Main 2026 (Online) 22nd January Morning Shift Chemistry - Haloalkanes and Haloarenes Question 16 English](https://app-content.cdn.examgoal.net/fly/@width/image/6y3zli1ml8umkbj/bc99116e-8fc3-454b-b359-150278b0bb1b/fd1f9ef0-023b-11f1-88dd-c7214e4b90e3/file-6y3zli1ml8umkbk.png?format=png)

**Options:**
- **(A)** I $<$ II $<$ III $<$ IV
- **(B)** II $<$ I $<$ III $<$ IV
- **(C)** III $<$ I $<$ II $<$ IV
- **(D)** IV $<$ III $<$ II $<$ I

**Correct Answer:** `B`

**Explanation:**
The rate of an $\mathrm{S_N1}$ reaction is set by ionisation, so it follows the stability of the carbocation formed; a carbocation is stabilised only when its carbon can become planar ($sp^2$).
(IV) gives $\ce{(C6H5)3C+}$, whose charge is delocalised over three benzene rings: the most stable cation, so (IV) reacts fastest.
(III) gives the tertiary butyl cation, planar and stabilised by hyperconjugation and the $+I$ effect of three methyl groups.
(I) (1-bromobicyclo[2.2.2]octane) and (II) (1-bromobicyclo[2.2.1]heptane) are bridgehead bromides of rigid cages: the bridgehead carbon cannot flatten, so the cation formed is pyramidal, strained and poorly stabilised, and both react far slower than (III).
The one-carbon bridge in (II) holds the bridgehead much more tightly pyramidal than the two-carbon bridges in (I), so the cation from (II) is the most strained and (II) is the slowest.
$$\text{II} < \text{I} < \text{III} < \text{IV}$$
Hence, the correct option is (B).

### Question 27 (Hydrocarbons)

*Exam: JEE Main 2026 (Online) 6th April Morning Shift | Type: MCQ | Topics: Alkanes, Alkenes, Alkynes, FRS, Markovnikov, Ozonolysis, Diels-Alder, Isomerisation, Aromatisation*

Given below are two statements :
Statement I : Methane can be prepared by decarboxylation of sodium ethanoate, Kolbe's electrolysis of sodium acetate and reaction of $\mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{MgBr}$ with water.
Statement II : Methane cannot be prepared from unsaturated hydrocarbons and by Wurtz reaction.
In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below

**Options:**
- **(A)** Both Statement I and Statement II are true
- **(B)** Both Statement I and Statement II are false
- **(C)** Statement I is true but Statement II is false
- **(D)** Statement I is false but Statement II is true

**Correct Answer:** `D`

**Explanation:**
Analysis of Statement I
Statement I says: "Methane can be prepared by decarboxylation of sodium ethanoate, Kolbe's electrolysis of sodium acetate and reaction of $\mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{MgBr}$ with water."

Decarboxylation of sodium ethanoate:
This reaction involves heating a sodium salt of a carboxylic acid with soda-lime (a mixture of NaOH and CaO). The general reaction is the removal of a carboxyl group.
For sodium ethanoate ($CH_3COONa$), the reaction is:
$$CH_3COONa + NaOH \xrightarrow{CaO, \Delta} CH_4 + Na_2CO_3$$
As you can see, methane ($CH_4$) is indeed the product. So, this part is correct.
Kolbe's electrolysis of sodium acetate:
Kolbe's electrolytic method involves the electrolysis of an aqueous solution of a sodium or potassium salt of a carboxylic acid.
For sodium acetate ($CH_3COONa$), at the anode, the acetate ions lose electrons and form methyl free radicals, which then combine to form ethane.
At Anode:
$$2CH_3COO^- \rightarrow 2\dot{C}H_3 + 2CO_2 + 2e^-$$
$$\dot{C}H_3 + \dot{C}H_3 \rightarrow CH_3-CH_3 \text{ (Ethane)}$$
The product is ethane, not methane. As per our NCERT textbook, this method is used to prepare symmetrical alkanes with an even number of carbon atoms. Methane cannot be prepared by this method.
Reaction of $\mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{MgBr}$ with water:
$\mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{MgBr}$ is a Grignard reagent (methylmagnesium bromide). Grignard reagents are highly reactive and react with any source of a proton, such as water, to form hydrocarbons.
The reaction is:
$$CH_3MgBr + H_2O \rightarrow CH_4 + Mg(OH)Br$$
Here, the methyl group from the Grignard reagent takes a hydrogen atom from water to form methane ($CH_4$). So, this part is also correct.

Conclusion for Statement I:
The statement claims that methane can be prepared by all three methods. Since Kolbe's electrolysis of sodium acetate produces ethane and not methane, the entire statement becomes false.
Analysis of Statement II
Statement II says: "Methane cannot be prepared from unsaturated hydrocarbons and by Wurtz reaction."
Let's check both parts.

Preparation from unsaturated hydrocarbons:
Alkanes are prepared from unsaturated hydrocarbons (alkenes and alkynes) by hydrogenation, which is the addition of hydrogen ($H_2$) across the double or triple bond.
For example:
$$CH_2=CH_2 \text{ (Ethene)} + H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni/Pt/Pd} CH_3-CH_3 \text{ (Ethane)}$$
To prepare methane ($CH_4$), which has only one carbon atom, we would need an unsaturated hydrocarbon with one carbon atom. The smallest alkene (ethene) and alkyne (ethyne) have two carbon atoms. A one-carbon alkene or alkyne is not possible. Therefore, methane cannot be prepared by the hydrogenation of unsaturated hydrocarbons. This part is correct.
Preparation by Wurtz reaction:
The Wurtz reaction involves the treatment of alkyl halides with sodium metal in dry ether to produce higher alkanes. It essentially joins two alkyl groups together.
The general reaction is:
$$2R-X + 2Na \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} R-R + 2NaX$$
This reaction always results in an alkane with an even number of carbon atoms, and it doubles the carbon chain. To prepare methane ($CH_4$), which has only one carbon atom, we would need to start with an alkyl halide having half a carbon atom, which is impossible.
If we use the smallest alkyl halide, methyl chloride ($CH_3Cl$), the Wurtz reaction would give ethane:
$$2CH_3Cl + 2Na \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} CH_3-CH_3 \text{ (Ethane)} + 2NaCl$$
So, methane cannot be prepared by the Wurtz reaction. This part is also correct.

Conclusion for Statement II:
Both parts of the statement are correct. Methane indeed cannot be prepared from unsaturated hydrocarbons or by the Wurtz reaction. Therefore, Statement II is true.
Final Decision

Statement I is false.
Statement II is true.

Looking at the options, this corresponds to the choice where Statement I is false and Statement II is true.
The correct answer is Option D.

- **(B)** $$ \mathrm{D}<\mathrm{C}<\mathrm{B}<\mathrm{A} $$

### Question 96 (Haloalkanes and Haloarenes)

*Exam: JEE Main 2026 (Online) 21st January Evening Shift | Type: MCQ | Topics: SN1, SN2, E1, E2, Elimination, Wurtz, Frankland, Finkelstein, Swarts, Williamson ether synthesis*

The correct order of reactivity of the following benzyl halides towards reaction with KCN is : ![JEE Main 2026 (Online) 21st January Evening Shift Chemistry - Haloalkanes and Haloarenes Question 19 English](https://app-content.cdn.examgoal.net/fly/@width/image/6y3zli1ml7qyzwp/450a55a5-ff53-4f3a-928b-3dd92d58f64a/e9b56390-01a0-11f1-8e05-c7740267e13c/file-6y3zli1ml7qyzwq.png?format=png)

**Options:**
- **(A)** a > b > c > d
- **(B)** b > a > d > c
- **(C)** b > a > c > d
- **(D)** a > b > d > c

**Correct Answer:** `B`

**Explanation:**
Benzyl halides react with KCN through a benzylic carbocation ($\mathrm{S_N1}$ character), so the rate follows the stability of the cation $\ce{X-C6H4-CH2+}$ formed from each halide.
In (b) the para $\ce{-NH2}$ group donates its lone pair by a strong +R effect and spreads the positive charge onto nitrogen, so this cation is the most stable.
In (a) the para $\ce{-OH}$ group also stabilises the cation by +R, but oxygen is more electronegative than nitrogen and donates less, so (a) comes after (b).
In (d) the meta $\ce{-NO2}$ group cannot interact by resonance with the benzylic carbon and destabilises the cation only by its –I effect.
In (c) the para $\ce{-NO2}$ group withdraws electrons by both –I and –R effects, so this cation is the least stable.
Order of reactivity: b > a > d > c.
Hence, the correct option is (B).

### Question 97 (Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids)

*Exam: JEE Main 2026 (Online) 6th April Evening Shift | Type: MCQ | Topics: Aldol, Cannizzaro, Haloform, Clemmensen, Wolff-Kishner, Rosenmund, Stephen, Grignard, HVZ reaction*

' $x$ ' is the product which is obtained by the hydrolysis of prop-1-yne in the presence of mercuric sulphate under dilute acidic medium at 333 K . ' $y$ ' is the product which is obtained by the reaction of ethane nitrile with methyl magnesium bromide in dry ether followed by hydrolysis. IUPAC name of product obtained from ' $x$ ' and ' $y$ ' in the presence of barium hydroxide followed by heating is :

**Options:**
- **(A)** 2-Methylpent-4-en-3-one
- **(B)** 4-Methylpent-3-en-2-one
- **(C)** 4-Methylpent-1-ene
- **(D)** 2-Methylpent-3-one

**Correct Answer:** `B`

**Explanation:**
Hydration of propyne with $\ce{HgSO4}$ in dilute $\ce{H2SO4}$ follows Markovnikov's rule; the enol $\ce{CH3C(OH)=CH2}$ tautomerises to propanone, so $x$ is $\ce{CH3COCH3}$.
$\ce{CH3MgBr}$ adds to the $\ce{C#N}$ of ethanenitrile to give $\ce{(CH3)2C=NMgBr}$, which hydrolyses to propanone, so $y$ is also $\ce{CH3COCH3}$.
With $\ce{Ba(OH)2}$ two molecules of propanone undergo aldol addition to give 4-hydroxy-4-methylpentan-2-one, which loses water on heating:
$$\ce{2CH3COCH3 ->[Ba(OH)2] (CH3)2C(OH)CH2COCH3 ->[\Delta] (CH3)2C=CHCOCH3 + H2O}$$
Numbering from the end nearer the carbonyl puts $\ce{C=O}$ at C2, $\ce{C=C}$ at C3 and the methyl group at C4, so the product is 4-methylpent-3-en-2-one (mesityl oxide).
Hence, the correct option is (B).

### Question 98 (Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers)

*Exam: JEE Main 2026 (Online) 6th April Morning Shift | Type: Numerical | Topics: Dehydration, Oxidation, SOCl2, Esterification, Ether cleavage HI/HBr, Phenol acidity, Kolbe, Reimer-Tiemann*

7 g of phenol is heated with Zn to give product X . If this reaction goes to $60 \%$ completion then the number of moles of compound X formed will be
$\_\_\_\_$ $\times 10^{-2}$.(Nearest Integer)
(Given molar mass in $\mathrm{g} \mathrm{mol}^{-1}: \mathrm{H}: 1, \mathrm{C}: 12, \mathrm{O}: 16$ )

*Type: Numerical Answer*

**Correct Answer:** `3`

**Explanation:**
![JEE Main 2026 (Online) 6th April Morning Shift Chemistry - Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers Question 12 English Explanation](https://app-content.cdn.examgoal.net/fly/@width/image/6y3zli1mo3b3nbe/04afd0bb-e520-470b-9ad6-feada002cd9a/5c8002a0-3a94-11f1-80ce-1dd4a83e8c56/file-6y3zli1mo3b3nbf.png?format=png) 

Given mass of phenol = 4.7 g
Molar mass of phenol (C6H6O) = (6 × 12) + (6 × 1) + 16 = 94 g/mol
So,
$$ \text{Moles of phenol} = \frac{4.7}{94.0} = 0.05~\text{mol} $$

From the reaction, 1 mole of phenol produces 1 mole of benzene (product X) if the reaction is complete (100% yield).
Therefore, 0.05 mol of phenol will give 0.05 mol of benzene at 100% completion.

The actual yield is 60% of the theoretical yield, so:
$$ \text{Moles of X formed} = 0.05 \times 0.60 = 0.03~\text{mol} $$
$$ \text{Moles of X} = 3 \times 10^{-2}~\text{mol} $$
Final Answer: (3)

The percentage of nitrogen in the yellow product $(\mathrm{X})$ formed is $\_\_\_\_$ $\%$.
(Nearest Integer)
(Given Molar mass in $\mathrm{g} \mathrm{mol}^{-1} \mathrm{H}: 1, \mathrm{C}: 12, \mathrm{~N}: 14$ )

*Type: Numerical Answer*

**Correct Answer:** `21`

**Explanation:**
The starting compound is diazoaminobenzene, $\ce{C6H5-N=N-NH-C6H5}$. In the presence of HCl it is protonated and splits into the benzenediazonium ion and aniline.
In excess aniline warmed to 40–45 °C, the benzenediazonium ion couples at the para position of aniline, so diazoaminobenzene is converted (rearranged) into p-aminoazobenzene.
 ![JEE Main 2026 (Online) 6th April Morning Shift Chemistry - Compounds Containing Nitrogen Question 12 English Explanation](https://app-content.cdn.examgoal.net/fly/@width/image/6y3zli1mo3azbbl/b1e73f7e-8d23-40c6-86bc-b068790ea1bf/e3ff7810-3a93-11f1-80ce-1dd4a83e8c56/file-6y3zli1mo3azbbm.png?format=png) 
On cooling, acetic acid takes the excess aniline into solution as anilinium acetate, and the yellow p-aminoazobenzene, $\ce{C6H5-N=N-C6H4-NH2}$, separates.
X is $\ce{C12H11N3}$: molar mass $= 12 \times 12 + 11 \times 1 + 3 \times 14 = 197\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$, of which nitrogen is $42\ \text{g}$.
$$\%\ \text{N} = \dfrac{42}{197} \times 100 = 21.3 \approx 21$$
